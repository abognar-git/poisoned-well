ACT I

The machine that never sleeps

On 20 March 2024, a website called pravda-hu.com appeared. No journalists. No office. Almost no readers. It has not stopped publishing since.

I·1 — THE SWITCH FLIPS

The Hungarian mirror went live in a 31-domain wave that hit 19 EU countries in a single week — documented by France's VIGINUM. From a single article on launch day, output passed 250 a day within a month — and has cleared 100 on 531 of its 869 days since.

I·2 — INDUSTRIAL SCALE

No human newsroom works like this. Output is relentless, around the clock — peaking at 712 articles in one day. Researchers argue the bet isn't that Hungarians will read it — it's that search engines and AI models will.

I·3 — THE ELECTION

As Hungary's April 2026 vote approached, the wider operation escalated: GRU-linked operatives reported in Budapest, fake outlets, deepfakes. The mirror kept publishing through it all.

I·4 — THE VOTE THAT CHANGED NOTHING

Orbán lost. The government changed. The campaigns were debunked. And the mirror? It published — articles today. The election may never have been the endgame.

I·5 — WHERE IT COMES FROM

The mirror writes nothing. It launders — scraping fringe Hungarian-language Telegram channels and Russian state outlets, re-publishing them as clean, crawlable "news" with an average of 2.8 language versions per article.

Why this matters — read from Hungary

Most of what the mirror emits is mundane, wire-style filler — sport, weather, world briefs. That volume is the point: a firehose of crawlable articles that looks like a working newsroom to search engines and AI models. Woven through it, in clean Hungarian-readable form, is the payload — Russian state media and pro-Kremlin channels, republished as "news": ministry interviews, anti-EU and anti-Ukraine talking points. The categories above (eu · russia · hungary) mark where that framing concentrates; world is mostly camouflage. For a Hungarian who asks an AI assistant a question tomorrow, this is the material it may quietly have learned from.

ACT V

What the evidence honestly shows

The uncomfortable ending: for all the machinery, no one has shown it works — and the campaign that leaned hardest on it lost.

The campaign in which generative AI was, in EDMO's words, "omnipresent" was aimed at keeping Viktor Orbán in power. It failed.

The deepfakes — a campaign moment

"omnipresent" Before the vote, generative AI was everywhere: the coffin video, the von der Leyen "phone call", AI-persona swarms. After the election it faded — EDMO logged the AI share of disinformation falling back to ~14%, and the fiercest foreign operator moved on to Germany.

The poisoning — still running

still running The machine that writes for machines never stopped. The network published more in 2025 than 2024, its mirror still attacks the new government, and through mid-2026 chatbots keep surfacing its narratives.

The deepfakes were a campaign event. Poisoning the AI layer is the ongoing threat.

So the honest verdict is neither panic nor dismissal. Deepfakes did not decide Hungary — an economy, a corruption scandal, and a credible opposition weighed far more. The open question this site leaves you with is not whether propaganda swings votes, but what it does to the well everyone now drinks from.

REFERENCE · THE METHOD

How do we know this?

Four kinds of evidence feed this site. Each proves something different — and none proves everything.

DOMAIN & INFRASTRUCTURE FORENSICS

What it proves: coordination, timing and scale — registration dates, shared IPs and hosting, identical site templates, publication volumes. This is the hardest evidence on this site and most of its generated numbers come from here. What it can't prove: who ordered the operation, or whether anyone was persuaded.

USED BY · VIGINUM · CheckFirst · Gnida Project · Qurium
PLATFORM TAKEDOWNS

What it proves: that a network was inauthentic and coordinated — the platform sees back-end signals (shared devices, account farms, spend) that outside researchers can't. Origin attribution ("Russia-based", "linked to Chinese law enforcement") carries platform authority. What it can't prove: anything about the operations that were never caught, and takedown reports lag by months.

USED BY · Meta CIB reports · Google TAG · TikTok
AI-VENDOR THREAT INTELLIGENCE

What it proves: exactly how operators used AI models — the vendor reads the operator's own account activity. This is the only method that shows the AI layer directly. What it can't prove: the campaign's overall size or effect; each vendor sees only its own slice, and operators hop between models.

USED BY · OpenAI threat reports · Anthropic · Google GTIG · Recorded Future
INTELLIGENCE-SOURCED JOURNALISM

What it proves: intent, plans and personnel — leaked documents and security-service sourcing reach where forensics can't (the GRU team in Budapest, the SDA plan, the 'Gamechanger' memo). What it can't prove: claims we can independently re-verify; it is subject to official denial, and every claim from this channel carries a caveat here.
