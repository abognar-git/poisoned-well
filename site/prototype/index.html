ACT I

A newsroom with no newsroom

On 20 March 2024, a domain called pravda-hu.com was registered. It publishes today as hungary.news-pravda.com — the network's Hungarian mirror. It has no newsroom and files no reporting of its own, and it has published on — of the — days since.

I·1 — THE SWITCH FLIPS

The Hungarian mirror went live in a 31-domain wave that hit 19 EU countries in a single week — documented by France's VIGINUM. From a single article on launch day, output passed 250 a day within a month — and has cleared 100 on 534 of its 873 days since.

I·2 — INDUSTRIAL SCALE

No human newsroom works like this. It peaks at 712 articles in one day. And by its own filing, only 4.0% of it is about Hungary at all — the rest is world filler and Russia. Researchers who have audited the network read that shape — vast output, almost no readers — as a bet on search engines and AI models rather than on Hungarian readers. It is their assessment of the design, and a peer-reviewed test has since disputed how far the effect generalises.

I·3 — THE ELECTION

As Hungary's April 2026 vote approached, the wider operation escalated: GRU-linked operatives reported in Budapest, fake outlets, deepfakes. The mirror kept publishing through it all.

I·4 — AFTER THE VOTE

Orbán lost and the government changed, and the influence campaigns were judged largely ineffective — most fabrications were pre-empted or debunked within days. The mirror's own output fell by nearly two thirds over the following two months, then climbed back; it published — articles —, and it is still publishing now.

I·5 — WHERE IT COMES FROM

The mirror writes nothing of its own. It launders: it takes fringe Hungarian-language Telegram channels and Russian state outlets and re-publishes them as clean, crawlable "news", carrying an average of 2.8 language versions per article so the same item is reachable in several languages at once.

The deepfakes were the loud part of it: they were seen, they were debunked, and after the vote the generative surge receded. ACT IV

How this content reaches the crawlers

Five stages from a fringe Telegram post to an AI assistant's answer, the process researchers call LLM grooming. The first three stages are measured in this repository; the last two are documented by outside audits and are marked as assessments. We have run no model-side test of our own, and the § markers say which is which.

Almost nobody reads the well directly; the crawlers do, and roughly 40,000 of the network's English-language articles have been documented sitting in Common Crawl, one of the corpora models are built from. What no one has traced is a specific article travelling from this mirror into a specific answer.

ACT V

What the evidence honestly shows

Two things are true at once, and the second does not follow from the first. No study has yet demonstrated that AI-enabled disinformation measurably changed the result of any election, and the campaign that used AI most heavily in Hungary lost. Losing is not evidence that the machinery failed, and a null result is not an all-clear; what follows is what this evidence will and will not carry.

It was a campaign in which EDMO assessed generative AI to be "omnipresent", it was aimed at keeping Viktor Orbán in power, and it lost.

The deepfakes — a campaign moment

"omnipresent" Before the vote: the coffin video, the von der Leyen "phone call", AI-persona swarms. After the election it faded — EDMO logged the AI share of disinformation falling back to ~14%, and the fiercest foreign operator moved on to Germany.

The poisoning — still running

still running The mirror never stopped, and the network published more in 2025 (~6.3M articles) than in 2024 (~3.6M). Its Hungarian mirror still attacks the new government, and through mid-2026 audits kept finding chatbots surfacing Russian state and sanctioned-outlet content. Whether that content reaches those answers by way of mirrors like this one is the researchers' assessment, and it is the part nobody has yet traced end to end.

V · i — The control group

The network runs one mirror per country, which gives this study something a single case normally cannot have: six sibling mirrors on the same infrastructure over the same months, none of them facing a Hungarian election. So we can ask whether what the Hungarian mirror did, the network did too. The six are not interchangeable with it — they launched at different dates, in different languages, into different news cycles — so they bound the answer rather than settle it.

What the collapse was made of

Rates, not percentages: a small-base source can post a large percentage change while moving under two articles a day.

V · ii — What Hungary built itself

Most of it was made at home

The foreign operations are the easier story to tell, and they were not the larger one. Four acts have asked what Russia did to Hungary; this one measures what Hungary's own machine did, and then tests rather than assumes whether the two were connected.

The pipeline test · 141,909 articles

If the domestic machine and the Russian machine were one machine, the Russian mirror should be crediting Hungarian pro-government output somewhere across its 141,909 articles. It never does — though what this measures is the mirror's own credit label, and a story laundered through an intermediate repost would be invisible to it by construction.

Counted · single digits, so no percentages

The clearest case

The conscription lie was built twice.

Storm-1516 pushed it through six purpose-built websites and Facebook ads reaching over 20,000 Hungarians. Separately, Fidesz's youth wing clipped a general's video to strip the words "today, the situation in Hungary is not such that" — and the truncated quote went into a government pamphlet to pensioners, then into a National Resistance Movement AI video. Two production lines, one fabrication, and — in the source's own words — no evidence that Fidesz is behind the Russian campaign.

Not computable — and we won't fake it

So the propaganda aimed at Hungarian voters was overwhelmingly Hungary's own, and where a pipeline to the Russian operation could be tested there is none. The two sides were recorded using four of the same techniques — targeted advertising, invented personas, smears and AI-generated images — but two unrelated sets of this size would overlap by about five, so we report that as a null and not as a finding. What the year actually showed is how cheap the toolkit had become: a governing party, a foreign intelligence-linked operation and one unfunded retiree in Debrecen could each afford the whole of it.

The verdict this evidence supports is narrower than either panic or dismissal. Nobody has shown that AI disinformation decided this election or any other, and nothing here establishes what did decide it; that is a question about Hungarian politics, and this site does not measure it. What we can say is that the loud part of the operation was answered — it was debunked, it receded, and the side it favoured lost — while the quiet part was never interrupted for long, and its output keeps accumulating in the open web corpora that models are built from. Whether any of it reaches a given answer is the part nobody has traced end to end.

REFERENCE · THE METHOD

How do we know this?

Four kinds of outside evidence feed this site, and each proves something different. A fifth is our own: the counts, comparisons and controls computed in this repository from CheckFirst's published scrape, which are marked as live data and can be re-run from the scripts.

DOMAIN & INFRASTRUCTURE FORENSICS

What it proves: coordination, timing and scale — registration dates, shared IPs and hosting, identical site templates, publication volumes. This is the hardest evidence on this site and most of its generated numbers come from here. What it can't prove: who ordered the operation, or whether anyone was persuaded.

USED BY · VIGINUM · CheckFirst · Gnida Project · Qurium
PLATFORM TAKEDOWNS

What it proves: that a network was inauthentic and coordinated — the platform sees back-end signals (shared devices, account farms, spend) that outside researchers can't. Origin attribution ("Russia-based", "linked to a St Petersburg contractor") carries platform authority. What it can't prove: anything about the operations that were never caught, and takedown reports lag by months.

USED BY · Meta CIB reports · Google TAG · TikTok
AI-VENDOR THREAT INTELLIGENCE

What it proves: exactly how operators used AI models — the vendor reads the operator's own account activity. This is the only method that shows the AI layer directly. What it can't prove: the campaign's overall size or effect; each vendor sees only its own slice, and operators hop between models.

USED BY · OpenAI threat reports · Anthropic · Google GTIG · Recorded Future
INTELLIGENCE-SOURCED JOURNALISM

What it proves: intent, plans and personnel — leaked documents and security-service sourcing reach where forensics can't (the GRU team in Budapest, the SDA plan, the 'Gamechanger' memo). What it can't prove: claims we can independently re-verify; it is subject to official denial, and every claim from this channel carries a caveat here.

About this prototype

Design-direction build: all five acts plus a reference glossary.
